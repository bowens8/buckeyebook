1. Weekly Picks

Every synced matchup gets a point spread. Pick a side and put money on it — everyone's wagers for that game go into one shared pot for that matchup only.

Placing, changing, and cancelling a pick

  • You can place one pick per game.
  • You can change your side or your wager amount, or cancel entirely for a full refund, anytime before kickoff.
  • The instant the game starts, your pick is frozen — no exceptions, and this is enforced by the database itself, not just the app's buttons, so it can't be worked around.

Against the spread (ATS)

You're betting whether your team beats the spread, not just whether they win. A team that's +7 only needs to lose by 6 or fewer (or win outright) to "cover." A team that's -7 needs to win by more than 7.

Settlement

Payouts happen on their own — the moment a game's final score comes in, every pick on it settles and pays out automatically. Nothing to wait on from the commissioner.

2. How Payouts Are Calculated

Weekly picks don't pay flat odds — how much you win depends on how emphatically you were right. A squeaker cover pays less than a backdoor blowout, and correctly calling an outright upset pays the most of all.

Step 1 — Which tier

TierConditionBase multiplier
1Underdog covers, loses outright1.0x
2Underdog covers and wins outright2.0x
3Favorite covers and wins1.0x
4Favorite wins but fails to cover0.5x flat (no margin scaling)

Step 2 — Scale by margin

Within tiers 1-3, the payout scales up the further you beat the spread by:

cover_margin = your team's actual margin − the spread they needed to beat scaling = 1 + (cover_margin / |spread|), capped at 3.0x final_multiplier = tier_base × scaling

Tier 4 is the one exception — since you didn't actually cover, there's no positive margin to scale by, so it's always a flat 0.5x regardless of how close or far the miss was.

Step 3 — Split the pot

Every winning pick gets weighted by wager × final_multiplier. The whole pot (minus any pushes, which are refunded in full) is split among winners proportional to their weighted stake:

your payout = pot × (your weighted stake / sum of all winning weighted stakes)
Example: You take a +7 underdog for $20. They win outright by 3 — that's Tier 2 (base 2.0x). Cover margin = 7 + 3 = 10, scaling = 1 + (10/7) = 2.43x, so your final multiplier is 2.0 × 2.43 ≈ 4.86x. Your weighted stake is $20 × 4.86 ≈ $97 — you're now competing for a much bigger slice of the pot than someone who just barely covered with the favorite.

Pushes

If the actual margin lands exactly on the spread, that's a push — full refund, no multiplier, doesn't touch the pot split for anyone else.

Buckeye stickers 🌰

Land a multiplier of 2.0x or higher (i.e. you called a real upset, not just a cover) and you earn a leaf sticker, shown next to your name on the Standings page. Purely bragging rights.

3. Line Locking

The point spread for weekly picks moves with the market right up until 48 hours before kickoff, at which point it locks permanently. This mirrors a real sportsbook's opening line vs. closing line — you're never picking against a number that quietly moved on you after you locked in.

  • Before the 48-hour window: the spread can shift as new lines come in.
  • Inside the 48-hour window: whatever the line is gets frozen and never changes again, even if the commissioner re-syncs that week for other reasons.
  • You can still place, change, or cancel your pick right up until kickoff — it's only the number itself (the spread) that locks 48 hours early, not your ability to act on it.
  • None of this needs a commissioner to trigger anything — games appear, spreads move and lock, scores update, and finished games settle entirely on their own in the background.

4. Live In-Game Bets

Once a game's underway, anyone can propose a side bet on anything happening in it — "next drive ends in a punt," "they score before the 2-minute warning," whatever. Every bet has two independent settings:

Who it's against

  • Open pool — anyone in the group can join either side, no invite needed.
  • Group challenge — you pick specific people; each one has to individually accept or decline before it counts. Declining or ignoring it doesn't block anyone else in the group from accepting.

How it pays out

  • 50/50 (even money) — no odds to set. Everyone's stakes go into one pot, winners split it proportional to how much they put in. Fully self-balancing — you can never pay out more than what was actually wagered.
  • Odds-based — the proposer sets odds (e.g. 3:1) and is effectively the house for that bet, taking the other side of everyone who accepts.

Resolving a bet

The commissioner marks the outcome once it's known (proposer's side won, or the field won), and payouts run automatically. Bets can only be resolved while they're still open.

5. In-Game Odds Can Move — Fairly

Unlike the weekly spread, live bet odds are never locked — the proposer can move the posted number at any point while the bet is still open, the same way a sportsbook line drifts during a game.

But moving the line never rewrites a deal someone already made: the moment you accept an odds-based bet, whatever number was posted right then gets frozen onto your own bet, permanently. If the proposer moves the odds afterward, it only affects people who join after that point.

Example: A bet is posted at 3:1. Spencer accepts — he's locked at 3:1 no matter what happens next. The proposer then moves the line to 2:1 because the situation's shifted. Joey accepts after that — he's locked at 2:1. When the bet resolves, Spencer gets paid at 3:1 and Joey at 2:1, even though it's the same bet.

The weekly pick spread and live bet odds are completely separate systems on purpose — the spread is the one number every pregame pick settles against, so it has to stop moving 48 hours out. Live odds are a conversation happening in real time during the game, so they stay fluid until the bet is settled.

6. Standings & Leaderboard

Two numbers track your money, and they mean different things:

  • Current balance — what you actually owe or are owed right now, shown in the header. This can go negative — you're not blocked from wagering just because you're already down. It only resets when the commissioner marks you "settled up" after real money actually changes hands.
  • All-time net — a permanent running total of every win and loss, shown on the Standings page. This is what the leaderboard ranks on, and it's never reset by a settle-up — it's the full historical record, independent of payout cycles.

Settling up

When you actually pay or get paid in the real world, the commissioner hits "Settle Up" next to your name. That zeroes out your current balance only — your all-time leaderboard position doesn't move. This lets the group cash out periodically (end of week, end of season, whenever) without ever losing the historical bragging-rights record of who's actually been the sharpest picker all year.

Your personal transaction history is on the same page, showing every wager, refund, payout, and settle-up individually.

7. Offline Mode

If the commissioner is somewhere without signal — tailgate, stadium, dead zone — they can still log bets and outcomes by hand from the Offline Entry page. Those entries save locally on their device and sync automatically the moment it reconnects. No one else can see or act on them until that sync happens.

One trade-off: offline entries skip the normal "can this person actually afford this" check, since that check needs a live connection. The commissioner is trusted to get the numbers right when working offline.

8. Accounts & Commissioner Access

  • Everyone logs in with a name and password — real accounts, so you can log in from any device.
  • Commissioner powers (syncing games, settling bets, resolving live bets, offline entry) require the commissioner code, entered at signup or unlocked later from the Standings page.
  • Ask whoever set up the pool if you need the code.
